Explanation · Particle identity

Particles without permanent species labels

A point can be real without carrying a permanent tag saying “electron” or “muon.” In the identity-based proposal, positions belong to the actual world; masses, charges, and internal alternatives belong to the wave dynamics. Familiar species behavior can become definite when the relevant packets separate.

This is an explanation of the proposal in the thesis, especially §5.9, “Distinguished particles”. It is a possible formulation, not evidence that nature has only one fundamental species. Read on to the bundle and statistics construction, or jiggle two initial positions and follow their outgoing roles. Background: the guiding equation.

What is actual, and what distinguishes a species?

In this version of Bohmian mechanics, the actual configuration is an unordered set of distinct points, \(Q_t=\{X_1(t),\ldots,X_N(t)\}\). A temporary numbering helps us draw continuous worldlines, but the numbering is not extra physical data. A point has a location and a trajectory. Its motion is guided by the wave, which can encode several masses and responses to external fields.

Three claims should be kept separate:

  • Identical points: exchanging arbitrary numerical names changes no configuration or law.
  • Different wave channels: the Hamiltonian can still distinguish internal sectors with different masses, charges, or spin spaces.
  • Effective species: in a sufficiently isolated channel, a trajectory obeys that channel's familiar law, making a species description useful.

“All points are fundamentally alike” therefore does not say that all wave channels have the same dynamics. The diversity has been moved into the wave and Hamiltonian. Deriving the actual list of masses and charges would require additional physics.

Two particles: add currents before dividing

Let an ordered auxiliary wave be \(\psi(x,y)=f_A(x)f_B(y)\). The channels have masses \(m_A,m_B>0\). Write \(a=|f_A|^2\), \(b=|f_B|^2\), and their individual guiding velocities as

\[u_s(x)=\frac{\hbar}{m_s}\operatorname{Im}\frac{\nabla f_s(x)}{f_s(x)},\qquad s=A,B.\]

At the unordered configuration \(\{x,y\}\), both assignments must enter: A at \(x\), B at \(y\), and the reverse. Define

\[D=a(x)b(y)+b(x)a(y),\qquad w=\frac{a(x)b(y)}{D}.\]
\[v_x=w\,u_A(x)+(1-w)u_B(x),\qquad v_y=w\,u_B(y)+(1-w)u_A(y).\]

Swapping \(x,y\) changes \(w\) into \(1-w\) and swaps the two velocities. Thus the law descends to the unordered configuration space. The displayed \(D\) is a density on that quotient with its locally Euclidean volume. Its normalized symmetric lift to the entire ordered space is \(D/2\); that common factor cancels from the guiding law.

This is a density-weighted average, not \((u_A+u_B)/2\) at every configuration. It is also not coherent symmetrization of a scalar wave: \(|f_A(x)f_B(y)+f_B(x)f_A(y)|^2\) generally has interference terms absent from \(D\). Here the alternatives occupy orthogonal bundle components.

Each product density has its own continuity equation. Adding the two gives a continuity equation for \(D\) with current \(D(v_x,v_y)\). With the requisite regularity and global flow control, this supplies equivariance. The distinction between a local identity and global existence still applies.

Packet separation makes one assignment effective

If A's packet is confined near \(x\) and B's near \(y\), then \(a(x)b(y)\) dominates the reverse assignment and \(w\) is close to one. The points follow approximately \(u_A(x)\) and \(u_B(y)\), independently of their temporary names. Exact disjointness gives the exact reduction wherever \(D>0\).

\[v_x-u_A(x)=(1-w)\,[u_B(x)-u_A(x)].\]

This identity gives a useful error criterion: small unwanted weight must be assessed together with the difference between channel velocities. Gaussian tails never vanish exactly. Their negligible influence is an approximation, and its persistence depends on continued separation and the Hamiltonian.

During overlap, one worldline need not have a unique species description. A nearby initial configuration can lead the continuously tracked point into the other outgoing packet. Deterministic evolution has not failed: the initial condition has changed. The channel data have not changed either. The exploration demonstrates this with exact free waves and numerically integrated trajectories.

In its fixed one-A/one-B sector, the other worldline acquires the complementary outgoing role. Changing which path becomes A-like does not change those populations or describe a physical conversion of one species into another.

When does a recognizable subsystem emerge?

Species separation and subsystem isolation are related but distinct. A few points may acquire clear channel behavior while remaining entangled with distant points. A subsystem with its own approximately autonomous wave also needs suitable factorization or environmental branch separation and weak residual coupling.

Our free product example supplies a limited instance: its Hamiltonian already decouples the two channels, and a dominant assignment factorizes into the two independent packets. While that dominance persists, the current error above controls the departure from their separate one-point guiding laws. This is an explicit approximation to two autonomous one-point systems, rather than a derivation of all subsystem physics.

Dürr, Goldstein, Tumulka, and Zanghì's On the Role of Density Matrices in Bohmian Mechanics, §6, equations (31)–(33), treats effective subsystem waves and conditional density matrices. Conditioning on the actual environment is not the same operation as merely tracing it out. With internal degrees of freedom, a conditional density matrix can be appropriate when a single conditional wave is unavailable.

The two-point experiment below has no apparatus or environment. It demonstrates the emergence of distinct channel-guided motions from overlap, not a complete account of decoherence, measurement records, or an autonomous laboratory system.

The published result and its limits

Sheldon Goldstein, James Taylor, Roderich Tumulka, and Nino Zanghì, Are All Particles Identical?, J. Phys. A 38 (2005), 1567–1576: journal article · free preprint. §§3–5 formulate the symmetrized law and bundle representation; §7 gives a direct internal-space route. §8 argues for empirical equivalence in quantum equilibrium despite different trajectories.

The thesis supplies the earlier setup and a broader mixed-species bundle proposal. Neither source derives the observed particle spectrum or establishes a general mechanism for creating species. The empirical-equivalence argument uses complete experimental records encoded in configurations; equality of a two-point snapshot distribution alone is not a proof about every multi-time experiment. Our perturbation experiment is not a proposed way to distinguish the two formulations experimentally.
