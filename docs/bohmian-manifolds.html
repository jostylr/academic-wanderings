Explanation · Quantum geometry

Bohmian motion on a manifold

To move on a curved space, a particle needs a tangent velocity at its current position. A wave can supply that vector directly. The same metric that defines its kinetic equation turns the wave's phase variation into a direction of motion.

This explanation develops the geometric core of chapter 3 of James Taylor's Connections with Bohmian Mechanics. The later joint paper Quantum Mechanics in Multiply-Connected Spaces, §5, is a published successor. Here the geometric construction, the checks it permits, and its analytic assumptions are kept separate.

Reading path: start here, continue to vector bundles and topological factors, then test three descriptions on a circle. The literature review compares this route with operator and quantization approaches. Background: tangent spaces and connections.

Specify geometry, a wave, and a current

Take a smooth Riemannian configuration manifold \((Q,g)\), a mass \(M>0\), a real potential \(V\), and a smooth scalar wave \(\psi(q,t)\). Initially work away from boundaries and singularities. Choose the Laplace–Beltrami sign for which \(\Delta e^{ikx}=-|k|^2e^{ikx}\) in Euclidean coordinates. The equations are

\[i\hbar\partial_t\psi=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2M}\Delta_g\psi+V\psi, \qquad \dot Q_t=v^{\psi_t}(Q_t),\qquad v^\psi=\frac{\hbar}{M}\operatorname{Im}\frac{\operatorname{grad}_g\psi}{\psi}.\]

The quotient is defined where the wave is nonzero. The actual configuration \(Q_t\) is one point of the manifold; \(q\) denotes a possible configuration. No global chart or ambient embedding is needed to say what a tangent vector or an integral curve is.

For a many-particle configuration space, unequal masses can be kept as separate kinetic coefficients or incorporated into a mass-weighted product metric. The published paper's mass-weighted convention absorbs factors that we display as \(1/M\). The configuration manifold need not be physical three-dimensional space.

Coordinates describe the law; they do not define it

In a local chart, write \(g=\det(g_{ij})\). The inverse metric raises the derivative index:

\[\Delta_g\psi=\frac{1}{\sqrt g}\partial_i\!\left(\sqrt g\,g^{ij}\partial_j\psi\right), \qquad (v^\psi)^i=\frac{\hbar}{M}g^{ij}\operatorname{Im}\frac{\partial_j\psi}{\psi}.\]

Repeated indices are summed. An angular coordinate velocity and the physical speed along an arc are different quantities; the metric relates them. Under a chart change these components transform as a vector field, so descriptions of a path agree on overlapping charts.

A global momentum observable is not an ingredient of this guiding equation. We have chosen a kinetic differential expression, however, and it still needs a self-adjoint realization to define quantum wave evolution. The review explains that distinction through the half-line example.

Probability uses geometric volume

Set \(\rho=|\psi|^2\) and \(j=(\hbar/M)\operatorname{Im}(\bar\psi\operatorname{grad}_g\psi)\). Probability is \(\rho\,d\mathrm{vol}_g\), with \(d\mathrm{vol}_g=\sqrt g\,d^nq\) locally. Volume can be treated as a density on a nonorientable manifold.

\[\partial_t\rho+\operatorname{div}_g j=0,\qquad \operatorname{div}_g j=\frac{1}{\sqrt g}\partial_i(\sqrt g\,j^i),\qquad j=\rho v^\psi.\]

The identity follows by subtracting the complex-conjugate Schrödinger equation; the real potential cancels. It is the local reason that the wave density and particle transport fit together. For global equivariance, probability must not be lost through a boundary, a node reached by an ending trajectory, or escape to infinity. The Jacobian review gives the transport side of this argument.

A smooth wave on a sphere, with a nonglobal velocity formula

On a sphere of radius \(R\), use polar angle \(\theta\) and azimuth \(\varphi\). The metric is \(R^2(d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2)\). For the free Hamiltonian, a normalized mode is

\[\psi(\theta,\varphi,t)=\sqrt{\frac{3}{8\pi R^2}}\sin\theta\,e^{i\varphi}e^{-iEt/\hbar}, \qquad E=\frac{\hbar^2}{MR^2}.\]

The spatial factor is the restriction of \((x+iy)/R\), so it is a smooth function even at the coordinate poles. Substitution gives \(\Delta_g\psi=-2\psi/R^2\). Its guidance law is

\[\dot\theta=0,\qquad \dot\varphi=\frac{\hbar}{MR^2\sin^2\theta},\qquad |v|_g=\frac{\hbar}{MR\sin\theta}.\]

Each nonpolar configuration stays on its latitude and has a trajectory for every time. The wave vanishes at the poles: the failure of the velocity quotient there is a real nodal issue, not just a poor chart. The two polar points have zero wave probability. This example establishes its own trajectories explicitly; it is not a global-existence proof for arbitrary wave fields.

What is established, and what has to be supplied?

Separate levels of the construction
ClaimStatus and conditions
The PDE and ODE make geometric senseEstablished from a metric, potential, and admissible smooth wave. The current is coordinate independent where the wave is nonzero.
The local continuity identityEstablished for smooth solutions with real potential. It does not by itself prove a global probability-preserving flow.
A unitary wave evolutionRequires a self-adjoint Hamiltonian and its domain. Complete-manifold theorems cover useful classes; boundaries and singular/incomplete spaces need separate choices.
Almost every path exists for all timeRequires estimates excluding finite-time loss at nodes, singular sets, or infinity. The topology papers explicitly leave a general existence analysis aside.
All quantum models on Q have been selectedNot established by these equations. The kinetic operator, curvature terms, bundle, connection, domains, and sector data are modeling choices.

Braverman, Milatovic, and Shubin's essential-self-adjointness results address wave evolution on bundles. Teufel and Tumulka's Simple Proof for Global Existence of Bohmian Trajectories supplies current-based sufficient conditions and applications, including spin and magnetic fields. Its stated configuration-space hypotheses must be checked; it is not a theorem about every arbitrary manifold. Smooth local geometry and global analytic control are distinct achievements.
