Quantum mechanics on manifolds: choices, operators, and trajectories

There are good wave operators on manifolds. What is harder is choosing a quantum model uniquely from classical or geometric data—and deciding what replaces a preferred list of Cartesian observables. Bohmian mechanics makes the particle law direct, while sharing much of the wave-operator analysis.

This is a review of several approaches, including the comparison in §2 of Quantum Mechanics in Multiply-Connected Spaces. Its criticism of a compulsory momentum-observable framework has a concrete mathematical example. Its broader claims about the clarity of competing interpretations are methodological arguments, not no-go theorems against quantum mechanics on curved spaces.

The approaches ask different starting questions

What is being constructed?
ApproachStarting pointWhat remains to choose or prove

Canonical/observable quantizationClassical observables and desired commutator rules.A usable observable subalgebra, ordering, representations, and self-adjoint domains.

These approaches can be combined. Geometric quantization might help select a Hamiltonian whose wave then guides a Bohmian configuration; confinement might supply an effective potential in either interpretation. They are not all rival claims about what a particle is.

Which operator is missing?

A global real coordinate need not exist, and a manifold need not have Euclidean translation symmetries. This prevents treating Cartesian momentum formulas as a universally preferred list of observables. It does not prevent position probabilities or Hamiltonians.

For sections in \(L^2(Q,E)\), position has a projection-valued measure: \((P(B)\psi)(q)=1_B(q)\psi(q)\) for measurable sets \(B\subset Q\). Real bounded functions on \(Q\) act as bounded self-adjoint multiplication operators. The 2014 quantum-triple preprint explicitly retains this position data alongside Hilbert space and Hamiltonian.

Momentum needs a more careful statement. A chosen real vector field \(X\) supplies the formally symmetric expression

\[\widehat p_X=-i\hbar\left(\nabla^E_X+\tfrac12\operatorname{div}_gX\right)\]

on compactly supported smooth sections, with the compatible Hermitian connection. This is one geometric generator associated with a chosen direction field, not a unique set of globally commuting momenta. Symmetry still does not establish a self-adjoint operator; completeness and domain issues enter.

The half-line makes the distinction sharp. The minimal \(-i\hbar\,d/dx\) on \(C_c^\infty(0,\infty)\) has unequal deficiency indices \((1,0)\), so it has no self-adjoint extension. The positive half-line Laplacian does admit self-adjoint extensions, such as the Dirichlet Hamiltonian. The absence of that momentum operator is therefore not the absence of a quantum theory. See Bonneau, Faraut, and Valent, §§5.2 and 6.2.

Intrinsic wave equations work, but do not select themselves

A metric defines the scalar Laplace–Beltrami operator; a bundle and compatible connection define a connection Laplacian. This is ordinary differential geometry used in standard spectral theory as well as Bohmian mechanics. On differential forms, after aligning sign conventions, Hodge and connection Laplacians can differ by a curvature endomorphism; the 2007 paper notes this in §5.4.

Even a scalar classical kinetic energy does not settle every lower-order quantum term. For example, the family

\[H_\xi=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2M}\Delta_g+V+ \frac{\hbar^2}{2M}\xi\,\mathrm{Scal}_g\]

has the same principal kinetic symbol for different real \(\xi\). Writing down covariant equations alone does not decide this parameter. The added term is a real potential, so the local current formula remains the same; the evolving waves and resulting paths can nevertheless differ. A physical model or a quantization prescription must supply the missing choice.

For analytic existence, there are strong positive results. A useful smooth subcase on a complete boundaryless Riemannian manifold is a Hermitian connection Laplacian with a smooth Hermitian potential bounded below. Braverman, Milatovic, and Shubin's Theorem 2.13 gives essential self-adjointness under its completeness, semiboundedness, and regularity assumptions; smooth coefficients meet the relevant local regularity requirements. The theorem is not a statement that arbitrary singular potentials on incomplete spaces have a unique realization.

Quantizing observables is a stronger demand

The Groenewold–van Hove problem concerns simultaneously assigning operators to a large classical Poisson algebra while retaining specified algebraic, commutator, and irreducibility properties. Obstructions already occur for the full polynomial algebra on flat phase space. They do not say that a selected Hamiltonian and useful observables cannot be defined.

Mark Gotay's Obstructions to Quantization explains both no-go and positive examples. Use the theorem's precise axioms and observable algebra when invoking it. A bare phrase such as “there are no suitable operators on a manifold” overstates the conclusion.

Geometric quantization makes different choices visible. It starts from a symplectic phase space, not just a configuration metric. A prequantum line bundle has curvature tied to the symplectic form and exists subject to an integrality condition. A polarization reduces the state space. Some classical flows do not preserve that polarization; additional constructions are then needed to represent their evolution.

Andrea Carosso's Geometric Quantization, §§3–4, is a free review with examples and a discussion of BKS evolution. Its prequantum bundle over phase space should not be confused with the configuration-space amplitude bundle in the Bohmian construction. The two can be related, but they serve different roles.

An embedded constraint can remember more than the intrinsic metric

Consider an ambient three-dimensional quantum particle squeezed into a thin layer around a surface. Under the specified confining limit, an effective surface Hamiltonian can acquire the geometric potential

\[V_{\rm geom}=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2M}(\mathcal H^2-\mathcal K),\]

where \(\mathcal H\) is mean curvature and \(\mathcal K\) is Gaussian curvature. R. C. T. da Costa's Quantum mechanics of a constrained particle, Phys. Rev. A 23 (1981), 1982–1987 (accessible paper copy), derives this in a physical confinement model.

Mean curvature depends on the embedding, so isometric surfaces can have different effective quantum models. This is evidence that the physical way a constraint is imposed matters, rather than evidence that intrinsic geometry is inconsistent. Bohmian mechanics can use the resulting effective Hamiltonian, but choosing a guiding law does not remove the confinement calculation.

What is especially direct in the Bohmian description?

Once the geometric wave model is chosen, its current provides a tangent vector field and the actual configuration follows an ODE. That makes the role of configuration space and a test for equivalent descriptions particularly explicit. One can compare projected paths and probability measures without first demanding a momentum operator for every coordinate.

The circle is a concrete success: a lifted twisted wave, a periodic wave with a connection, and a twisted self-adjoint interval domain give the same motion when matched correctly. More general topological sectors appear by demanding a descended current and compatible wave evolution. The topological-factors page states the scope and the higher-rank conditions.

This directness concerns the formulation. The wave PDE still uses a Hamiltonian and its domain. At nodes, the current-to-density quotient is undefined. At boundaries, singularities, or infinity, trajectories can require a global analysis. Even the smooth sphere example has zeros of its wave. Thus “PDE plus ODE on a manifold” is a clear starting point, not an automatic well-posedness theorem.

For an operator comparison, an explicit equivalence should map domains and preserve position as well as the Hamiltonian. A unitary \(U\) should obey \(U\mathcal D(H)=\mathcal D(H')\), \(UH=H'U\), and \(UP(B)=P'(B)U\). The 2014 preprint develops this quantum-triple viewpoint. Our local frame and circle transformations additionally preserve the displayed guiding current.
