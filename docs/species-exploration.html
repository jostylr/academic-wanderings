Exploratory · Particle identity

Jiggle a configuration, change its outgoing role

Begin with two points inside the same overlapping spatial cloud. Keep the wave fixed. A small sideways change in one point's initial position can send its continuous trajectory into the other outgoing packet.

This experiment implements the two-assignment current in the identity explanation. It uses two spinless channels in a physical plane, one A and one B, with \(\hbar=1\), masses \(m_A=1,m_B=4\), initial position standard deviation \(\sigma=0.7\), and packet-center velocities \(u_A=(2,0),u_B=(-2,0)\). These masses and velocities are inputs. The experiment exhibits effective channel behavior; it does not create new particles, derive the species spectrum, or simulate exchange statistics.

What to look for

  1. Compare the two small-jiggle buttons at \(t=3\). Only the initial x-coordinate of the round point changes, from \(-0.04\) to \(+0.04\). The same wave channels produce B-like and A-like outgoing motion, respectively.
  2. Scrub time back to zero. Both spatial densities initially coincide and both assignment weights are one half. Distinct outgoing packet phases and masses are already present in the wave. No point is secretly allocated a channel by the display.
  3. Choose the balanced configuration. It remains equally weighted: the model does not promise that every initial condition acquires a definite species role.
  4. Compare \(\delta=0.002\) and \(\delta=0.12\). Later dominance can be sharp while the finite-time flow remains continuous in the initial data. The outcome label uses a 99% threshold; it is not a discontinuity in the differential equation.

Reference calculation at \(t=3\), rounded to three decimals: with \(\delta=+0.04\), the round point is near \((3.174,-1.369)\); with \(\delta=-0.04\), near \((-4.855,-0.600)\). The unwanted assignment weights are below \(10^{-20}\) in these runs. At exactly zero displacement, its position is near \((-1.804,-0.906)\) with weight \(1/2\). These are numerical ODE results for specified configurations, not experimental data or probabilities for an ensemble of jiggles.

The waves are exact free solutions

For each channel \(s\), set \(r_s=x-u_st\), \(\tau_s=t/(2m_s\sigma^2)\), and use the normalized two-dimensional Gaussian

\[f_s(x,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}(1+i\tau_s)} \exp\!\left[-\frac{|r_s|^2}{4\sigma^2(1+i\tau_s)}+im_s\left(u_s\cdot x-\frac{|u_s|^2t}{2}\right)\right].\]

It satisfies \(i\partial_tf_s=-\Delta f_s/(2m_s)\). Its density and guiding velocity are

\[a_s(x,t)=\frac{\exp[-|r_s|^2/(2s_s^2)]}{2\pi s_s^2},\quad s_s^2=\sigma^2(1+\tau_s^2),\qquad U_s(x,t)=u_s+\frac{t\,r_s}{4m_s^2\sigma^4+t^2}.\]

The product \(\psi(x,y,t)=f_A(x,t)f_B(y,t)\) solves its unequal-mass free two-body equation. Its two assignment components give \(D=a_A(x)a_B(y)+a_B(x)a_A(y)\), and the actual two-point velocity is the weighted law on the explanation page. A weight is a share of this local current construction; it is not a sampled hidden species or, by itself, a probability for every possible later measurement.

The plot shows physical space. The full configuration has four coordinates, and each point's assignment weight depends on both positions. “Two packets have separated” must be understood together with this configuration dependence.

What the computation establishes

The wave evolution above is analytic. Trajectories use fourth-order Runge–Kutta with time step \(0.005\); the time control chooses the nearest computed snapshot. Independent finite differences check the Schrödinger equations, phase-gradient velocities, and summed continuity equation. Step-halving checks verify the quoted trajectories and their separation.

In this particular model, the Gaussian assignment density is strictly positive. The velocity is smooth even across the coincidence set and is bounded by \(C_T(1+|q|)\) on each finite time interval. This excludes finite-time escape. The coincidence set is invariant by permutation symmetry, so uniqueness also prevents a distinct-point solution from reaching it in finite time. These properties justify a global flow for this free Gaussian model; they do not settle singular or interacting models.

Gaussian packets retain tails, and the perfectly balanced solution never becomes a pure assignment. There is no collapse of the full wave. For a laboratory subsystem, stable environmental records and decoupling would need to be added; see the subsystem discussion.

Here the channel Hamiltonians are already free and decoupled. When one assignment dominates over an interval, the factorized packets supply approximately independent one-point systems; the displayed speed error measures the tracked point's instantaneous departure from its dominant channel law. Both opposite-jiggle runs retain one A and one B channel occupation. What changes is which worldline acquires each outgoing role.

Where this leads

The two-assignment setup is the thesis's §5.9 construction specialized to exact free packets. The related published formulation is Goldstein, Taylor, Tumulka, and Zanghì's Are All Particles Identical? (2005), §3, equations (10)–(11). The packet parameters and numerical example here are an independent worked example.

A useful next experiment would bring separated channels back into overlap and test when species-like tracking ceases to be effective. A third point introduces more assignments and collective dependence. Neither extension would derive masses or exchange signs: the bundle explanation distinguishes those inputs from packet selection.
