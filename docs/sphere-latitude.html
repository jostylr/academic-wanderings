Where the two Taylor polynomials part on a sphere

Unroll a narrow strip of longitude and latitude onto graph paper and give the paper its Euclidean metric. A horizontal line is straight on the paper. Wrapping it onto the unit sphere makes it a circle of latitude. This map lets us compare four derivatives by two independent calculations.

A flat strip mapped onto a curved patch

Use the map \[f(u,w)=(\cos w\cos u,\cos w\sin u,\sin w),\qquad |w|<\pi/2.\] Fix \(p=(0,\phi)\), write \(h=\cos\phi\), \(s=\sin\phi\), and take the source line \(\gamma(t)=(t,\phi)\). Its image begins at \(q=(h,0,s)\). An orthonormal basis at \(q\) is east \(E=(0,1,0)\) and north \(B=(-s,0,h)\). All the pairs below are components in this fixed tangent basis. The parameter \(t\) is the change in longitude, in radians.

Take the spherical logarithm first

Put \(c(t)=f(t,\phi)\). The central angle \(\theta\) from \(q\) to \(c(t)\) satisfies \(\cos\theta=q\cdot c(t)=s^2+h^2\cos t\). The initial direction of their great circle is the tangent projection of \(c(t)\), normalized. Multiplying by the arc length \(\theta\) gives \[\log_q c(t)=\frac{\theta}{\sin\theta} \big(h\sin t,\;hs(1-\cos t)\big).\] At \(t=0\) the prefactor is one by continuity. For the east component use \(\sin t=t-t^3/6+O(t^5)\). For the north component use \(1-\cos t=t^2/2-t^4/24+O(t^6)\). Both use \(\theta/\sin\theta=1+h^2t^2/6+O(t^4)\). Reading the derivatives from the resulting polynomial gives the middle column below.

Differentiate the moving velocity instead

Along the latitude the unit east vector is \(E(t)=(-\sin t,\cos t,0)\) and the unit north vector is \(B(t)=(-s\cos t,-s\sin t,h)\). Differentiate in three dimensions and project onto the tangent plane. This gives \(D_tE=sB\) and \(D_tB=-sE\). The speed is \(h\), so start with \(\dot c=hE\) and apply these two rules successively.

Order Derivative of the logarithm Covariant derivative along the latitude
1 \(hE\) \(hE\)
2 \(hsB\) \(hsB\)
3 \(-hs^2E\) \(-hs^2E\)
4 \(hs(2h^2-1)B\) \(-hs^3B\)

At \(45^\circ\), \(h=s=1/\sqrt2\). The north component of the fourth logarithmic derivative is zero; the fourth covariant derivative is \(-1/4\). This example checks both the coefficient and sign of the paper’s curvature formula. At the equator the image is a great circle and both constructions give exactly \((t,0)\).
