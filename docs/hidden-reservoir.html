Musings · Particle identity

Can a hidden reservoir look like particle creation?

Suppose an instrument sees zero particles in a chamber, then sees two tracks emerge from a small region. What does the zero tell us? If the instrument responds only to particular internal states, the chamber could have contained the material for both tracks all along.

Consider a nonrelativistic universe of exactly \(N\) identical bosonic points. Give each location three internal wave channels, a dark channel \(D\) and two detectable channels \(+,-\). An ideal detector couples equally to empty space and to \(D\), but sends its pointer to distinct records for the two visible channels. This specifies what “invisible” means for this instrument. A conversion experiment can still reveal the reservoir indirectly.

What could switch on two tracks at one location?

Take the thesis’s tensor product of internal spaces indexed by positions in an unordered configuration \(q\). For simplicity take a common mass, trivial internal connection, and the same exchange statistics in every channel. On the factors at \(x,y\), let \(|S\rangle=(|+-\rangle+|-+\rangle)/\sqrt{2}\). Add the following Hermitian term to the Hamiltonian, with a real coupling \(g(r)\) supported inside radius \(R\):

\[V(q)=\sum_{\{x,y\}\subset q}g(|x-y|)\left(|S\rangle\langle DD|+|DD\rangle\langle S|\right)_{xy}.\]

The sum runs once over each unordered pair. The coupling is unchanged if the points exchange names. It takes two dark amplitudes into a correlated visible pair and also permits the reverse. Net charge can be conserved by choosing charges \(0,+e,-e\). In a closed, translation-invariant system, the incoming relative kinetic and internal energy must cover the outgoing threshold at the same total momentum. Different track energies can then carry information about the reservoir.

For one isolated pair held within range, degenerate channel energies and a constant matrix element \(g\) give an exact check. Starting in \(|DD\rangle\), the visible-pair probability at time \(t\) is \(\sin^2(gt/\hbar)\). Starting in \(|S\rangle\) gives the same probability for disappearance into \(DD\). Reversal in a collision requires the visible packets to come back within range in the coupled state. For an apparent single birth triggered by an existing visible point, a different pair term can implement \(A+D\leftrightarrow A+B\) wherever charges and energies permit it. These choices predict different count correlations.

The identity-based interpretation gives a particular account of the record. The actual configuration follows continuous point trajectories through the interaction region. While the internal amplitudes overlap, both channels contribute to the current. After sorting and recording, the occupied branch can contain two visible tracks. The first visible marks locate a conversion to the detector’s resolution. Assigning a precise instant at which a hidden species label jumped would require an extra dynamical rule. The published fixed-population construction supplies the underlying points and bundle. The conversion term here is a proposed extension. Goldstein, Taylor, Tumulka, and Zanghì, §§6–7 · Taylor, thesis, equation (5.9b).

How dense would the dark channel need to be?

In a uniform dark preparation of density \(n_D\), a tagged dark point has mean dark neighbor count

\[\lambda_R=n_D\int_{|r|<R}g^{(2)}_{DD}(r)\,d^3r.\]

Here \(g^{(2)}_{DD}\) is the pair correlation of the preparation. For uncorrelated positions, \(\lambda_R=4\pi n_D R^3/3\). In the Poisson limit, \(\lambda_R=0.1\) gives about a 9.5% chance of at least one neighbor, while \(\lambda_R=3\) gives about 95%. Thus a dense bath can make partners available throughout space. Suppressed close pairs can undo this even at a high mean density. The Poisson percentages apply only to the uncorrelated example.

Partner availability answers a geometric question. A transition rate also involves encounter duration, matrix elements, energy matching and the state of the outgoing modes. In a dilute uncorrelated gas of identical dark particles with independent binary encounters, the forward event rate per volume is approximately \(n_D^2\langle\sigma_{\rm conv}v_{\rm rel}\rangle/2\). The factor of two counts each incoming pair once. Extrapolating this law to many neighbors inside one interaction volume would require a many-body calculation.

A tension worth testing. A dense bath that converts readily without a trigger would light up an otherwise quiet detector. A reservoir can be plentiful yet quiet if the dark preparation lies below the reaction threshold, or if a collision with another excitation supplies the necessary energy and quantum numbers. Compare records with the excitation present and absent. Then repeat while depleting the bath near the source. Background counts, a change in pair yield, or a recovery time while dark particles flow back would each distinguish versions of the hypothesis.

When does the visible theory look like particle creation?

Restrict to three bosonic modes in one interaction region and denote their annihilation operators by \(d,a_+,a_-\). Projecting a pair conversion onto these modes can give

\[H_{\rm mix}=\kappa a_+^\dagger a_-^\dagger d^2+\bar\kappa(d^\dagger)^2 a_-a_+.\]

The coefficient includes the spatial overlap and normalization of the modes. Every forward step removes two dark occupations and adds one of each visible type. Thus \(N_D+N_++N_-\) is exactly conserved, and \(N_{\rm vis}=N_++N_-\) changes in steps of two. The visible vacuum \(|N,0,0\rangle\), with occupations listed in dark, plus, minus order, can produce the first pair. From a state with \(k\) visible pairs, the forward matrix element is

\[\langle N-2k-2,k+1,k+1|H_{\rm mix}|N-2k,k,k\rangle =\kappa(k+1)\sqrt{(N-2k)(N-2k-1)}.\]

For \(2k\ll N\) and large \(N\), the square root is approximately \(N\). On this ladder the observer obtains the same leading transition amplitudes as for a pair source of strength \(\kappa N\). For \(N=100\), the source factor at the tenth existing pair is about 80% of its initial value. The ladder ends at 50 pairs. The visible stimulation factor \(k+1\) also changes, so the 80% figure measures reservoir depletion alone. This comparison concerns amplitudes in the joint state and the resulting count statistics. In a fixed-total-number state, different visible totals accompany orthogonal dark number states. Tracing out the dark mode erases coherence between those totals from the visible density matrix. Phase-sensitive predictions require specifying access to a reference or a joint measurement.

A laboratory example already realizes the counting mechanism. Qu, Evrard, Dalibard, and Gerbier prepared sodium atoms in the \(m=0\) spin channel and measured correlated pairs appearing in \(m=+1,-1\) through spin-changing collisions. Selecting the two output channels makes the input population play precisely the reservoir role. In that experiment the input atoms are independently accessible. Extending this mechanism to a universally hidden population is the speculative step. Free experimental paper · Physical Review Letters publication.

The answer depends on which observations the model must reproduce. A finite reservoir can reproduce visible birth and death records exactly for the specified channel model. Over a low-depletion range it can simulate particular variable-number source dynamics. An arbitrarily high visible occupation exceeds its capacity. Matching a relativistic field theory would additionally require its propagation laws, vacuum correlations, reaction amplitudes, and symmetries. A material bath can introduce a detectable rest frame through velocity-dependent rates. In Bell-type quantum field theories, by comparison, the configuration itself can jump between number sectors. Agreement of one detector’s counts would leave the two trajectory descriptions underdetermined. Dürr et al., Bell-Type Quantum Field Theories.

A tractable next question is whether one finite-range coupling can give a low background, a large triggered yield, and repeatable outgoing records across a range of energies. Those three measurements together would say more about the hidden-reservoir proposal than density alone. Explore how a channel becomes a pointer record.
