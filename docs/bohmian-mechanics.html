How the wave function guides particles

In Bohmian mechanics, particles have positions that change with time. The wave function gives a velocity field on configuration space, and the actual configuration follows that field.

The guiding equation

Consider \(N\) spinless particles in three dimensions with a scalar potential \(V\). Their positions form a single configuration \(Q(t)=(Q_1(t),\ldots,Q_N(t))\). The wave function \(\psi_t\) evolves on configuration space according to Schrödinger’s equation.

\[ i\hbar\,\partial_t\psi_t = -\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_k}\Delta_k\psi_t + V\psi_t . \]

Write \(\nabla_k\) for the gradient in the \(k\)th particle’s coordinates and \(m_k\) for its mass. At configurations where \(\psi_t\) is nonzero, the guiding equation gives each velocity.

\[ \frac{dQ_k}{dt}(t) = \frac{\hbar}{m_k}\operatorname{Im} \left(\frac{\nabla_k\psi_t}{\psi_t}\right)(Q(t)). \]

For a wave function that couples the particle coordinates, the velocity of one particle can depend on the positions of the others.

Where probability enters

The velocity field is the quantum probability current divided by the density \(\rho_t=|\psi_t|^2\). Schrödinger’s equation with a real potential gives the continuity equation for this density.

\[ \partial_t\rho_t + \sum_{k=1}^N \nabla_k\cdot j_k = 0, \qquad j_k = \frac{\hbar}{m_k}\operatorname{Im} \bigl(\overline{\psi_t}\,\nabla_k\psi_t\bigr). \]

Where the flow is well defined, this is the same equation for transporting an ensemble of configurations along the guiding field. Under suitable conditions, an ensemble initially distributed by \(|\psi_0|^2\) therefore has density \(|\psi_t|^2\) at time \(t\). This preservation is called equivariance.
