Exploratory · Quantum geometry

One circle, three descriptions of the same motion

Put a twist into the wave, or put a connection into its derivative. Cut the circle somewhere else. When the data are matched, the energy and particle motion agree.

This exact rank-one example accompanies the topological-factors explanation. It tests equivalent descriptions of one chosen free mode. It does not test the full non-Abelian construction or prove a classification of arbitrary cut domains.

The mode and the conventions

Use \(\hbar=M=1\), a circle of radius \(R\), and integer mode label \(n\). The sector parameter \(\alpha\) is defined modulo one. With its energy time factor suppressed,

\[\widehat\psi(\theta)=C e^{i(n+\alpha)\theta},\qquad \phi(\theta)=e^{-i\alpha\theta}\widehat\psi(\theta)=C e^{in\theta},\qquad C=(2\pi R)^{-1/2}.\]

The cover wave has twist \(\gamma=e^{2\pi i\alpha}\). The periodic wave uses \(D_\theta=\partial_\theta+i\alpha\). Parallel transport for that connection has holonomy \(\gamma^{-1}\). Both descriptions give

\[E=\frac{(n+\alpha)^2}{2R^2},\qquad \dot\theta=\frac{n+\alpha}{R^2},\qquad v_{\rm arc}=R\dot\theta,\qquad \rho=\frac{1}{2\pi R}.\]

The particle starts at physical angle zero. Time is a snapshot parameter, not an automatic animation. The circle is drawn at a fixed display size; the radius control sets its physical scale. The dashed radial line marks an arbitrary coordinate seam; the marked point crosses it without reflection.

The cover's amplitude changes after a turn, but its density and current do not. The periodic-frame amplitude returns, but its derivative includes the connection. Omitting the connection while keeping the periodic components would describe another model.

Moving the seam changes a coordinate label, not the physical point, energy, or motion. The interval endpoints are glued, not walls. This is a direct check of the circle equivalence, not a proof of the thesis's general cut-independence proposal.

Predictions to try

  1. Choose \(n=0\), \(R=1\), and compare \(\alpha=0\) with \(\alpha=1/2\). The density is the same, but the chosen free mode changes from rest to angular speed one half.
  2. At fixed \(n,\alpha\), double \(R\). Energy and angular velocity fall by four; arc speed falls by two. The normalization changes with circumference.
  3. Keep the state and time fixed and move the seam. The seam-relative angle changes, while the physical dot and both velocities agree.
  4. Compare \((n,\alpha)=(1,0)\) and \((0,1)\). They represent the same \(n+\alpha\) and the same sector. Shifting \(\alpha\) by one relabels the spectrum; keeping \(n\) fixed is not the same state.

Source and limits of the model

The example specializes the scalar-cover circle construction in Dürr, Goldstein, Taylor, Tumulka, and Zanghì, §6.1–§6.2. The free mode is nodeless, so its circle trajectory exists explicitly for all times. It demonstrates normalization, gauge covariance, and gluing for this model; it does not remove the domain or nodal questions for general manifolds and bundles.
