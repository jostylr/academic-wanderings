Exploratory · Dirac equation

Read a velocity from a Dirac spinor

A positive density does not specify which way probability flows. Keep the density fixed, change a relative component phase, and watch a Dirac current change.

A small window into four components

Take a free massive Dirac state with momentum along \(z\), in the standard Dirac representation. Restrict to the spin-up sector with first and third components occupied. On these two components, the dimensionless momentum-space Hamiltonian is

\[\frac{H}{mc^2}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\kappa\\\kappa&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \kappa=\frac{p_z}{mc},\qquad \varepsilon=\sqrt{1+\kappa^2}.\]

Set \(r=\kappa/(\varepsilon+1)\), \(a=1/\sqrt{1+r^2}\), and \(b=ra\). A normalized positive-energy eigenspinor is \(u_+=(a,0,b,0)^T\). The sign of \(b\) follows the momentum. The first slider chooses this eigenstate; the second then applies a relative phase to its third component:

\[\chi(\kappa,\delta)=(a,0,b e^{i\delta},0)^T,\qquad \rho=\chi^\dagger\chi=1,\qquad \frac{v_z}{c}=2ab\cos\delta.\]

These are spinor factors of a plane wave, with a common spatial factor \(e^{ip_z z/\hbar}\) suppressed. The constant density is a convenient normalization, not a normalizable wave packet on all space.

Three comparisons

First leave \(\delta=0\) and change the momentum. The equation's positive-energy eigenspinor gives \(v_z/c=\kappa/\varepsilon\), matching \(dE/dp_z\) for \(E=mc^2\varepsilon\). It reverses with momentum, vanishes at rest, and approaches the speed of light in magnitude for large momentum.

Next set \(\kappa=1\) and turn the phase to \(90^\circ\). Neither component magnitude changes, so the density stays one. The current becomes zero. At \(180^\circ\) it reverses.

What happened to \(v_z=pc^2/E\)? Except for special cases such as zero momentum, the phase-adjusted state is no longer a positive-energy eigenstate. It is a superposition of positive- and negative-energy eigenspinors at that momentum. The positive-energy weight displayed above is the squared projection onto \(u_+\). The current is an instantaneous value; the relative phase will evolve under the free Hamiltonian. Changing this slider does not describe a particle maintaining that velocity forever.

Finally set the momentum to zero. The third component vanishes, so changing its phase has no effect. Every selected state obeys \(|v_z|\le c\), since \(2|ab|\le |a|^2+|b|^2=1\).

What this checks

The component formulas follow by diagonalizing the displayed two-by-two Hamiltonian and evaluating the four-component Dirac current. They illustrate the local-current construction; they are not a wave-packet evolution or an antiparticle detector. Compare the free solutions and current in David Tong's Quantum Field Theory, §§4.6–4.7.
