Explanation · Quantum geometry

When the wave lives in a vector bundle

A vector bundle lets the wave carry internal amplitudes without demanding one global basis for them. A connection compares nearby fibers; a Hermitian metric compares amplitudes in the same fiber. Together they define the probability current.

This extends the manifold explanation and develops chapter 3 of the thesis. The core construction appears in the 2007 joint paper, §5.4. It accommodates spin, gauge connections, and topologically nontrivial wave bundles, with further structures specified for each physical model.

What replaces a complex number?

A rank-\(k\) complex vector bundle \(E\to Q\) assigns a \(k\)-dimensional amplitude space \(E_q\) to each configuration. A wave is a section: \(\psi(q)\in E_q\). Locally we may write \(k\) complex component functions; a different local frame mixes them. On overlaps, those descriptions must agree by the bundle's transition maps.

A positive Hermitian product \(h_q\) gives the local density \(\rho=h(\psi,\psi)\). We take it conjugate-linear in its first slot and linear in its second. The global state Hilbert space is \(L^2(Q,E,d\mathrm{vol}_g)\), formed by integrating this density.

A connection \(\nabla^E\) differentiates a section in a tangent direction. We require it to preserve \(h\). This is extra geometric data, not something determined by a bare bundle alone. A topologically trivial bundle can still carry a connection with nontrivial holonomy.

One wave equation, one invariant current

Use the Levi-Civita connection of the fixed metric \(g\) on the base, the chosen Hermitian connection on \(E\), a mass \(M>0\), and a smooth fiberwise Hermitian endomorphism \(V\). Define the connection Laplacian with the same nonpositive sign as the scalar Laplace–Beltrami operator:

\[\Delta^E\psi=\operatorname{tr}_g(\nabla^2\psi),\qquad (\nabla^2\psi)(X,Y)=\nabla^E_Y\nabla^E_X\psi-\nabla^E_{\nabla^g_YX}\psi.\]

The newest differentiating direction is last, as in the site's Taylor conventions. Equivalently, in a local frame \(D_i=\partial_i+A_i\),

\[\Delta^E\psi=\frac{1}{\sqrt g}D_i\!\left(\sqrt g\,g^{ij}D_j\psi\right),\qquad i\hbar\partial_t\psi=\left(-\frac{\hbar^2}{2M}\Delta^E+V\right)\psi.\]

The current is most clearly specified by its contraction with an arbitrary real tangent vector \(X\):

\[g(j,X)=\frac{\hbar}{M}\operatorname{Im}h(\psi,\nabla^E_X\psi),\qquad \rho=h(\psi,\psi),\qquad v^\psi=j/\rho\quad(\rho>0).\]

The Hermitian connection makes the product rule work, and the Hermitian potential makes its contribution to \(\partial_t\rho\) cancel. The result is \(\partial_t\rho+\operatorname{div}_g j=0\). The PDE and particle ODE are intrinsic; global wave and trajectory existence still need the conditions discussed on the manifold page.

A changing frame does not change a path

In unitary frames, the connection matrices \(A_i\) are anti-Hermitian. If the components change by \(\psi'=U(q)\psi\), transform the connection with them:

\[A'=UAU^{-1}-(dU)U^{-1},\qquad D'\psi'=U D\psi.\]

Then both \(h(\psi,\psi)\) and \(h(\psi,D\psi)\) have the same values in both frames. For a time-independent frame change, transform \(V\) as \(UVU^{-1}\) and the Hamiltonians intertwine as well. Time-dependent changes require the extra time-connection/potential term; they are outside this displayed calculation.

Changing components while keeping an incompatible connection fixed changes the physical model. Gauge-equivalent descriptions are different from genuinely different connections. The circle exploration makes this distinction explicit with a removable local potential and a surviving loop phase.

Examples, and structures they do not determine

  • Scalar waves: the trivial complex line with its trivial connection recovers the scalar equations.
  • Electromagnetism: on a trivial line in a chart, \(D=d-ieA/\hbar\) gives mechanical phase gradient \(dS-eA\), for signed charge \(e\). A global line bundle and connection may be needed instead of a single global potential.
  • Spin: a rotation representation acts on internal amplitudes. A spinor bundle requires suitable spin or spin-c structure and a compatible connection. A generic rank-two Hermitian bundle is not automatically a spinor bundle. The spin explanation describes the magnetic coupling and its modeling choices.
  • Topology: tensoring with a flat unitary line bundle changes global gluing while keeping the same local differential expression. Higher-rank factors can carry non-Abelian data; the topology page states their compatibility conditions.

The displayed current is the convective current for this connection-Laplacian model. A divergence-free spin term can lead to another equivariant current. A choice motivated by the Dirac limit is discussed in the guiding-equation comparison. Bundle geometry alone does not establish uniqueness of every admissible guiding current.

What these results leave open for a particular model

The current construction and its local covariance are established, and the published topology papers extend the thesis's scalar factors. They do not prove that every bundle, connection, singular potential, or boundary condition gives a unique global Bohmian model. The thesis's broad cut-independence argument needs a domain-level reconstruction; a square-root motivation for the Dirac equation remains a motivation with extra assumptions, as its own explanation makes clear.

For a model under study, specify \((Q,g,E,h,\nabla^E,V)\), the self-adjoint operator domain, the regularity class of states, and any topological sector. Then check current covariance, probability balance, and trajectory existence. The review separates results supplied by the literature from checks still needed.
