Do force bosons need Bohmian paths?

Send one photon through a beam splitter and put a counter at each output. An ideal run ends with one counter firing. Quantum optics predicts which counter will fire with what probability. Does that click also tell us that a point of light traveled from the splitter to the counter?

The answer depends on what the theory puts in space between the source and the detector. There is a theorem against a particular sharp position observable for a free photon. There are also published models with photon trajectories, and models in which the only particles with positions are fermions. The same click can have a dynamical explanation in more than one of these pictures.

What does the localization theorem rule out?

Suppose every spatial region \(B\) has a yes-or-no test for “the photon is in \(B\) at this time.” For a sharp position observable, the tests are projections. Disjoint regions have orthogonal projections, all of space has the identity projection, and translating or rotating the apparatus carries the projection into the one for the moved region. On the usual physical one-photon space in three spatial dimensions, the massless helicities \(+1\) and \(-1\) do not admit such a position observable. This fixed-time obstruction already uses ordinary spatial symmetries. One need not demand that position be part of a Lorentz four-vector. Newton and Wigner, Localized States for Elementary Systems · Wightman, On the Localizability of Quantum Mechanical Systems.

One can see where a familiar construction goes wrong. A photon momentum amplitude \(f(\mathbf k)\) is transverse, so \(\mathbf k\cdot f=0\). Differentiating gives \(\mathbf k\cdot\partial_{k_i}f=-f_i\). The usual position derivative \(i\partial_{k_i}\) can take a physical amplitude out of the transverse space. This diagnoses the naive operator. The theorem addresses the more demanding question of whether any sharp observable has all the stated properties.

Probabilities survive. Momentum and helicity have ordinary Born probabilities. For a spatial measurement one can use positive operators \(F(B)\) with probabilities \(\langle\psi,F(B)\psi\rangle\). These operators sum to the identity over an exhaustive partition but need not be projections. Guarnieri, Motta, and Lanz obtain such an unsharp position observable by projecting a sharp observable in a larger space down to the transverse photon states. For a real counter one must also specify its coupling, bandwidth, aperture, and time window. Guarnieri, Motta, and Lanz, Single-Photon Observables and Preparation Uncertainty Relations.

For example, Glauber’s ideal weak, broadband absorbing detector has a count rate proportional to \(\langle E^{(-)}_e(\mathbf x,t)E^{(+)}_e(\mathbf x,t)\rangle\), where \(e\) selects the detected polarization and the two factors are the negative- and positive-frequency parts of the electric field. This predicts a response at a specified place and time. In a short time gate with negligible multiple counts, integrating the calibrated rate gives the click probability. It does not supply the position of an undetected photon at every earlier time. Glauber, The Quantum Theory of Optical Coherence.

Which curves deserve to be called photon paths?

In a Bohmian model the position is an additional physical variable. A candidate law needs both a velocity and a distribution transported by that velocity. It also needs an account of what happens when the light interacts with an apparatus. Several constructions answer different parts of that question.

Follow the energy. In vacuum a classical Maxwell wave has energy density \(u\) and Poynting vector \(\mathbf S\) satisfying \(\partial_t u+\nabla\cdot\mathbf S=0\). Where \(u>0\), curves with velocity \(\mathbf S/u\) transport the energy distribution in that frame. An energy fraction and a photon-count probability can differ even for one photon. Prepare equal amplitudes in two separated output modes centered at \(\omega\) and \(2\omega\). Ideal counters equally efficient at both frequencies register half the clicks in each arm. Calorimeters averaging the absorbed energies over many runs report twice as much energy in the higher-frequency arm. Its share of the energy is two thirds. The half-and-half count distribution and the one-third/two-thirds energy distribution describe different measurements.

Reconstruct a flow from measurements. Kocsis and colleagues weakly measured transverse momentum and then recorded detection positions. Repeating the preparation at a series of propagation planes gave conditional averages from which they reconstructed curves in a two-slit interference pattern. A single detection contributes to an ensemble average at one plane. Connecting those averages across planes does not follow the same photon through successive planes. Kocsis et al., Observing the Average Trajectories of Single Photons in a Two-Slit Interferometer.

Postulate a guided point. Kiessling and Tahvildar-Zadeh give a relativistic wave equation for one free photon in three spatial dimensions. They construct a conserved four-current \(J^\mu\) with nonnegative time component by contracting a tensor of the wave with a vector determined from the whole state. In coordinates \(x^0=ct\), \(\mathbf v=c\mathbf J/J^0\) transports the proposed density where \(J^0>0\). This adds a law for a possible photon position without constructing the forbidden sharp position observable. In a separate paper with Lienert, an electron and a photon in one spatial dimension interact through a boundary condition at coincidence. The authors give an explicit guiding law and analyze the resulting paths. The particle count is fixed. Neither result establishes the photon worldlines of full interacting quantum electrodynamics (QED) in three spatial dimensions, including emission and absorption. Free single-photon model · Interacting electron–photon model, §7.

How far does Are All Particles Real? take us?

In their 2005 paper, Goldstein, Taylor, Tumulka, and Zanghì ask which degrees of freedom in the wave function need actual positions. Their companion paper on identical particles asks whether the existing points need intrinsic species labels. These are independent choices. One can take the positions of the fundamental matter fermions, the quarks and leptons, as the material configuration while allowing the state guiding them to contain gauge-field degrees of freedom. Even a composite boson such as a helium-4 atom can then leave a track through the motion of its constituents and the matter in the detector. Are All Particles Real?, equations (1)–(5) · The separate question of particle identity.

Here is the argument in the paper’s fixed-particle Schrödinger setting. Write \(x\) for all retained coordinates, \(X(t)\) for their actual values, and \(y\) for the omitted coordinates. Assume ordinary nonmagnetic kinetic energies and a local Hermitian potential. With the internal indices contracted by the dagger, set

\[\rho(x)=\int \Psi^\dagger\Psi\,dy,\qquad J_i(x)=\frac{\hbar}{m_i}\int \operatorname{Im}(\Psi^\dagger\nabla_{x_i}\Psi)\,dy,\qquad \dot X_i=\frac{J_i(X)}{\rho(X)}.\]

Integrating the full continuity equation over \(y\) removes its divergence if the boundary flux vanishes. Thus \(\partial_t\rho+\sum_i\nabla_{x_i}\cdot J_i=0\). With a well-defined flow away from nodes, a configuration initially distributed as \(\rho\) keeps that marginal distribution. This averages currents before dividing by density. Simply erasing the \(y\)-paths from a trajectory computed in the original theory generally yields a different motion. The full interacting wave still evolves. A reduced matter state can be mixed, and its future generally depends on its correlations with radiation.

Return to the beam splitter. Let \(L(x)\) and \(R(x)\) be normalized wave packets for the retained apparatus particles with macroscopically disjoint supports, recording “left counter fired” and “right counter fired.” After an ideal one-count measurement, write the joint state as

\[\Psi(x)=a L(x)\,|r_L\rangle+b R(x)\,|r_R\rangle,\qquad |a|^2+|b|^2=1,\]

with normalized residual radiation states. At \(x\) in the left record only the first term contributes, so integrating the squared radiation-space norm over that region gives \(|a|^2\). The right record has probability \(|b|^2\). This works even when both final radiation states are vacuum after absorption. If the alternatives differ only in the radiation while the matter packets still coincide, there is no left or right pointer reading yet. A detector interaction has to produce that separation in the variables the theory actually keeps. Seoane, Benali, and Oriols illustrate the distinction with a single cavity mode, two confined electrons, and two pointers whose separated positions record which electron took up the excitation. Their simulation also guides a cavity-mode coordinate. The recorded outcome is already readable from the matter pointers. Two-detector calculation, §§5–6 and figure 3.

The paper therefore gives a criterion for dispensing with photon positions. In quantum equilibrium, the retained variables must encode how the experiment is carried out and which outcome is recorded. This criterion does not uniquely select all fermions. The authors even consider quarks alone or electrons alone, since either can reveal the arrangement of ordinary matter. Equal one-time matter distributions alone say little about a sequence of unrecorded paths. Recorded sequences can be compared through the final arrangement of the apparatus and its memory. Radiation still propagates, interferes, and transfers energy according to the joint dynamics. Extending the construction to QED requires a current appropriate to gauge coupling and a law for the chosen matter configuration when pair processes occur. The photon itself never needed an omitted position coordinate. In a field model the norm and inner product can be taken directly in the radiation Hilbert space.

What, then, is in space in a pilot-wave field theory?

  • Fermion occupations. Bell puts a fermion number at each lattice site. The quantum state, including its bosonic degrees of freedom, determines stochastic jumps between occupation configurations. In equilibrium the rates reproduce the quantum distribution of the configurations. A counter fires when its matter rearranges. The construction uses a spatial lattice and its time evolution. Bell, Beables for Quantum Field Theory.
  • Fermion trajectories. Colin and Struyve develop a deterministic Dirac-sea model with positions for the occupied fermions. A particle–antiparticle excitation can appear as an excited electron and a hole in the sea. They discuss spatial volume and ultraviolet cutoffs and restrict their construction to interactions conserving total fermion number. Extending it to the fermion-number-violating processes of the Standard Model requires further work. Colin and Struyve, A Dirac Sea Pilot-Wave Model for Quantum Field Theory.
  • A radiation field. Struyve and Westman instead give the transverse electromagnetic field an actual configuration guided by the QED state. Radiation scattered or emitted by different pointer arrangements can carry distinguishable field records. Here a field exists between source and counter, and the model assigns no worldline to each light quantum. The authors use the field records to explain how even a material pointer can be read. Struyve and Westman, A Minimalist Pilot-Wave Model for Quantum Electrodynamics.

Do the same arguments apply to gluons, W and Z bosons?

The photon theorem uses both zero mass and physical helicity. A massive free spin-one particle admits Newton–Wigner localization at fixed time. For the physical W and Z the practical issue is their short lifetime. Experiments reconstruct them from decay products. For gluons the issue is color confinement. An energetic gluon can initiate a jet whose observed constituents are hadrons and their decay products. There is no isolated asymptotic colored gluon whose position one could test like that of an incoming free photon. Neither observation proves a universal ban on hidden trajectories for gauge bosons. Particle Data Group, W and Z properties and decay modes · Particle Data Group, Quantum Chromodynamics, §9.3.

A virtual exchange line in a scattering calculation is a term in a perturbation expansion. It gives no measured itinerary for a little force carrier. A fermion-only model can keep the entire interaction that generates scattering amplitudes while attributing definite tracks to the source, target, and detector matter. Its strongest claim is conditional and concrete. If those variables carry all the records and the dynamics gives their quantum statistics, an additional gauge-boson position is unnecessary to account for those experiments. Whether a theory with such positions can also succeed is a separate dynamical question.
